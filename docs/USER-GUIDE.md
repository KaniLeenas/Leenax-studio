# User guide

How one subject goes from course documents to a published YouTube video. Every stage is a draft until a project owner or reviewer approves it, and the person who made a version can't approve it themselves. The dashboard's **Waiting for you** list and the bell tell you when something needs you.

## 1. Start a subject project

My projects → **New project** → University subject. Fill in the subject name, module code, academic year and semester; the studio warns you if a similar project exists. The owner adds the others under **People** on the project page, each with a role:

| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| Owner | everything in the project, including authorising YouTube publishing |
| Editor | upload, generate, edit and send for review |
| Reviewer | approve, ask for changes, reject |
| Viewer | read |

## 2. Course documents

**Documents**: upload PDFs, Word, PowerPoint, OpenDocument, text files or photos of pages. The document service reads them (with English and Tamil OCR for scans), splits them into pages and passages, and makes them searchable with page numbers. A new upload of the same document becomes a new version.

## 3. Syllabus and topics

- **Syllabus**: an AI report of the modules, outcomes and topics, with each statement linked to the page it came from, and contradictions between documents listed. Send it for review.
- **Topics**: once the syllabus is approved, edit the topic list (add, rename, reorder, merge, split, set difficulty and prerequisites) and send it for review. Everything after this works per approved topic.

## 3a. Topic analysis and automation

Open **Topic analysis** on the project page. Each topic in the approved topic list gets an analysis: what students should be able to do, the key ideas, the mistakes students commonly make, how many questions to write for each objective, and a scene-by-scene plan for the lesson video. Read it, edit anything that's wrong (the question plan must add up to the number of questions), and send it for review.

To have Leenax carry on by itself, choose **Automation settings**, tick the first box, and choose the AI account and model. Then approving the topic list starts the analyses, approving an analysis starts the notes, approving the notes starts the questions, and once the notes, QA report and SEO report are approved the presentation is made. You still review and approve every step, and you still start QA checks and SEO research yourself. The settings say how many AI requests this can make on your account.

## 4. Notes and questions

- **Notes and questions** → notes per topic at five levels (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, University, Exam), in an editor with tables, equations, code, diagrams and images. Sources are checked against the documents.
- **Question banks** with eight kinds of question; each multiple-choice question has four options and one key. **Quizzes** are drawn from a bank with a recorded seed. Downloads: PDF, Word, Excel, CSV.

## 5. Academic QA

**Academic QA** checks notes claim by claim against the course documents and answers the questions without seeing the keys. Issues are critical, major or minor; a fix is proposed and a person accepts or rejects it, then a recheck closes it. **Nothing with an open critical issue can be approved.** The QA report per topic is approved like any other stage.

## 6. YouTube SEO

**YouTube SEO** researches keywords through YouTube's own data (within the daily budget), imports Google Trends files you download, analyses public competitor videos and scores 12 to 15 titles. Every number shows where it came from or is marked "estimated". The approved SEO report is what later goes to YouTube: title, description, tags, playlists.

## 7. Presentations

**Presentations**: a deck from approved notes, once the topic's QA and SEO reports are approved. Edit slides, layouts, tables, diagrams and speaker notes; text that doesn't fit blocks approval. Export to PowerPoint and PDF.

## 8. Lesson and quiz videos

**Lesson and quiz videos**:
- A lesson video follows an approved presentation, narrated from its speaker notes. A quiz video reads an approved question bank, counts down and reveals each answer.
- Choose a voice, edit any narration, then **Make the narration**.
- **Render**. Every render is checked (length, picture, sound) before it counts. A video can be approved only with a render of exactly its content that passed.

## 9. Publish to YouTube

**Publish to YouTube** (owners and administrators):
1. Choose an approved video and **Publish**.
2. Pick the channel and one of the approved titles. The description and tags are the approved ones, shown exactly as they'll be sent.
3. Optionally: a thumbnail, playlists, captions (made from the video's own timing), and hashtags.
4. Visibility: private, unlisted, public, or scheduled to go public later. Upload now or at a set time.
5. Answer "made for kids" and the altered or synthetic content question.
6. **Your authorisation:** confirm you hold the rights to everything in the video, and confirm it will be public if it will. Your name, the time and a statement of what you authorised are recorded. Nothing uploads without this record, and changing the video or settings afterwards needs a new one.

The history shows each publication's status, its YouTube address and who authorised it. If YouTube's daily quota runs out, the upload waits and tries again.

## 10. YouTube analytics

**YouTube analytics**: choose a channel and dates, then **Refresh from YouTube**. Each refresh is kept as a snapshot you can reopen. You'll see views, watch time, view duration, retention per video (with its scenes), subscribers, likes, comments, shares, traffic sources and search terms. Impressions and click-through rate need **reach reports** turned on once per channel; the first arrives about two days later. Recommendations are rules applied to your own numbers, each showing its rule and figures.

## The dashboard and notifications

- **Waiting for you:** versions to review (not your own), changes asked for on your work, approved videos ready to publish (owners), and your failed AI jobs from the last week.
- **University work:** counts from the database for the projects you can see.
- **Running now** and **Recent decisions.**
- **The bell:** review requests, decisions on your work, finished or failed AI jobs, renders and posts. Click one to open it; it's then marked read.

## Creator workspace

The rest of the studio (AI video and image generation, voice, subtitles, thumbnails, the editor, the auto-editor and publishing to YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok) is described in the README phase by phase. Posting there also records an authorisation, and public posts need the confirmation box.
